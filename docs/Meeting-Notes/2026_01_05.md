## Discussions

- [HGR paper research notes](https://hackmd.io/@tJJO0b0UQYilXlfLdhiWaQ/BJ8bUma4Zl)
- Key findings:
  - Use of a lightweight MLP architecture based on contrastive learning.
    - Reduces the size of the data needed and the training timeframes.
  - Allow computer interaction with simple hand movements.
  - Range of applications of HGR for research on _project practical use case_.
  - **Challenge**: Convey to other systems or humans the intended meaning of user's gesture without having to teach them the new meaning for that gesture.
  - **Solution**: User-defined hand gestures
    - Each user has their own interpreter.
  - **Problem of previous studies**:
    - They don't address the problem that **_the definition of hand gesture may vary widely among users from different e.g. cultural backgrounds_**.
  - Their system takes into account how long it will take to collect the necessary data.
    - **GESTOP** takes around 15-20 minutes to complete data collection for a single hand gesture.
    - New system reduces the time to 5s at a frame rate of 18 fps, and use data augmentation techniques.
      - Data augmentation -> Rotation of video frames by 180 degrees about the origin and then scaled down by a factor of 0.8 to reduce image size.
  - **New architecture limitations**:
    - The system is based on static gestures only.
    - Lack of interactivity of the UI and lack of system security.
    - Users found it tedious to add user-defined hand gestures as they had to click multiple buttons.
    - Demand for a relatively large amount of user data.
    - **_Defining more than six gestures and providing training and test data was seen as demanding and challenging._**
    - Training conducted on static datasets.
      - Knowledge base of the model remains unchanged post-training.
        - Lack the ability to incorporate new information.
      - Model does not adapt or evolve in response to subsequent data.
    - System's inability to function without a camera.
